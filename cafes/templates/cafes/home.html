    Where your caffeine came from

    Before the flat white, there was a leaf, a goat, and a very long trade route.

    Around 2700 B.C.

    Tea becomes the first popular caffeinated drink, born from Chinese tea cultivation.

    Tea hits the road

    Buddhist monks bring tea seeds from China to Japan, while European missionaries and merchants carry tea leaves home.

    Ethiopia, thousands of years later

    Legend says a goat herder named Kaldi noticed his goats turning lively after eating coffee cherries, and coffee was discovered.

    The Arabian Peninsula

    Coffee arrives and spreads quickly across the world, helped along by the yearly pilgrimages to Mecca.

    Today, in your cup

    Experts say 3 to 5 cups a day (300 to 400 mg of caffeine) is generally safe for most adults, enough for a lift in energy and focus without the jitters or lost sleep. Everyone reacts differently, so listen to your body.

    Sources: American Pharmaceutical Review and Aroma Coffee.
